I actually like the brand direction quite a bit. The navy, mint, electric blue, orange, typography, and restrained UI all feel coherent. I would not redesign the brand.

The problem is that the page currently feels like 12 individually designed sections that happen to be stacked together, rather than one deliberate sales experience.

The content is pretty good. The storytelling architecture is what I'd change.

My diagnosis

Right now the page roughly does this:

Product → mechanism → pricing → features → another product screen → speed → integrations → industries → competitive advantage → dashboard → calculator → problem → calculator result...

That's why it feels disjointed.

The visitor has to repeatedly figure out:

“Why am I looking at this section now?”

A senior designer would make the entire page answer one progressively developing question:

“If I give Fynd my completed jobs, what happens, and why should I trust it enough to pay $97/mo?”

That's the story.

I would cut the page almost in half

I'd reorganize everything into five acts.

ACT 1 — The outcome

Get more Google reviews without asking for them.

Show the product working.

↓

ACT 2 — The mechanism

Here's exactly what happens after every job.

Job complete → text → customer → Google → response

↓

ACT 3 — The proof

Here's what that does to your reputation.

Reviews accumulate → rating improves → you outrank competitors

↓

ACT 4 — The fit / risk removal

It works with your existing workflow.

CRM integrations, no CRM, industries, setup.

↓

ACT 5 — The purchase

$97/mo. Here's exactly what you get.

Then CTA.

That would make the page feel dramatically more intentional.
